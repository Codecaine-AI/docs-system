    Anatomy of STE sentences

    annotated examples

    Original text, not STE: unapproved words in red

    It is imperative that the operator ensures the hydraulic reservoir is replenished prior to commencing operation.

    1 · Procedural sentence (the same instruction, rewritten)

    13 words, limit 20
    Make sure1 that the hydraulic reservoir2 is full3 before4 you5 start6 the operation.
    • 1Approved verb, command form
    • 2Technical name, 2-word noun cluster (max 3)
    • 3Not “replenished”
    • 4Not “prior to”
    • 5Active voice
    • 6Not “commencing”

    2 · Safety instruction

    WARNING:1 Do not touch the brake unit until it is cool.2 Hot parts can cause injury.3
    • 1WARNING = risk of injury. CAUTION = risk of damage.
    • 2First: a clear, simple command
    • 3Then: the risk

    3 · Descriptive sentence

    The pump supplies1 fuel to the engine when the switch is on.
    1 · Simple present tense12 words, limit 25 · max 6 sentences per paragraph

    Verb forms

    Section 3
    FormExampleStatus
    Command (imperative)Close the valve.Approved
    Simple presentThe valve closes.Approved
    Simple pastThe valve closed.Approved
    Simple futureThe valve will close.Approved
    InfinitiveTurn the knob to close it.Approved
    Past participle (adjective)The closed valveApproved
    Progressive (-ing)The valve is closing.Not approved
    PerfectThe valve has closed.Not approved
    Passive, in proceduresThe valve must be closed.Not approved
    Use the -ing form only inside a technical name, for example “landing gear”. Descriptive text can use the passive only when it is necessary.

    Dictionary entries

    Part 2 format
    Word (part of speech)StatusMeaning or alternativeApproved exampleNot approved
    Convention: approved words are shown in UPPERCASE, unapproved words in lowercase. An approved word keeps only its listed meaning and part of speech. “Close” is a verb only, never an adjective meaning “near”.
    E

    Writing rule limits

    maximum values
    • Use the same word for the same thing every time.
    • Do not leave out words like “the”, “a” and “this”.
    • Use the active voice in procedures.
    • Use vertical lists for complex text.
    • Write one topic in each paragraph.

    I

    Using STE with an LLM

    prompt patterns

    Strict

    Explain how our build cache works.
Write in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.

    Softened (spec is very strict for prose)

    Explain how our build cache works. Write about
80% of the way to ASD-STE100: short sentences,
one topic per sentence, active voice, the same
word for the same thing. Keep normal technical
vocabulary.

    WHY IT WORKS

    Models already know the spec. One short name in the prompt brings a full, consistent set of style rules.

    WHAT TO RELAX

    The ~900-word dictionary. Software words (cache, deploy, schema) are “technical names” anyway.
